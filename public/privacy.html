Keypals privacy notice

Keypals is a free learning game for kids: typing, math, and an adventure where your answers cheer up grumpy creatures. It's made by FancyAnt Games (Erik Wilder) and played at keypals.org (and fancyant.com/keypals). Contact: hello@fancyant.com. Last updated 29 October 2026.

In short
  • No ads and no tracking, and we never sell or share information.
  • We count visits (how many people play, and what they play) without knowing who anyone is: no names, save codes, addresses or ids, and no outside trackers. A grown-up can turn it off.
  • Children never give us an email, full name, birthday, photo, voice or location, and there's no chat.
  • Playing without saving online keeps everything in your own browser.
  • Saving online is something a grown-up turns on. It saves only a nickname and game progress. Grown-ups who sign in share their email and name from Google.
  • You can delete saved players and accounts yourself, any time, from the Grown-ups screen.

What we keep, and why

Just playing. Progress is kept in your browser on this computer and isn't sent to us. That covers the player's nickname, chosen buddy, typing lessons and math trips, stars, stickers, medals, the Adventure (creatures collected, eggs, shells, things to wear) and settings. Family Duel and Rival Ghosts use only the players on the same computer, and nothing about them goes online.

Saving online (optional, turned on by a grown-up). A grown-up can save a player online from the Grown-ups screen. That player gets a save code, like "tiger moon rain 42", to keep playing on another computer. We store that player's game profile: the nickname (we suggest a first name or a made-up name), buddy, progress and settings, plus when it was saved and last opened. Nothing else. If two computers play the same player, their progress is combined so nothing is lost. Anyone who has the code can open that player, so keep it like a library card.

Grown-up sign-in (optional, for parents and teachers). If you sign in with Google, we keep the email address and name Google shares, and which players you keep in your account. We don't get your password, contacts or anything else from your account. Children don't sign in. The Grown-ups screen and sign-in sit behind a short grown-up check. Signed in, your players are the same on every computer you sign in on.

Deleting a player everywhere. When a signed-in grown-up deletes a player "everywhere", the saved player is deleted and we keep only its save code and the time it was deleted (no nickname or progress), so other computers remove their copies too. That note is deleted after 3 years.

Counting visits. To know how many people play and what they play, the game counts, on your own computer, and sends us only the counts. It sends: that the game was opened, and on a phone, tablet or computer; for each player, "one more player today" the first time they play each day (and the same for the first time each week and month), and how many days since they started, in a few groups (so we can see whether players come back); which screens were opened; when a game ends, what kind of game it was, its subject and area, how many minutes it took (in groups like "3 to 5") and the stars; how many minutes were spent in games; and that a grown-up signed in. We add the country (only the country, from our hosting) to each opening. We store only daily totals, like "Tuesday: 41 players, 12 math trips". No names, save codes, network addresses, browser details or anything that could tell one player or computer from another are stored, and we keep the totals for two years. To turn it off for a computer, use "Don't count my visits" on the Grown-ups screen. If your browser asks sites not to track it (Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control), nothing is counted.

Ideas and help. Anyone can give an idea on the Ideas board a heart. We keep only how many hearts each idea has; which ideas you gave a heart to is remembered in your own browser. A grown-up can suggest an idea or tell us about a problem (behind the grown-up check). We keep what they write, and an email address only if they ask for a reply; we use it only to answer and delete it after 180 days. Ideas show on the board, without any email, only after we've read them. When something breaks in the game, it can send us a short problem report by itself: the error message, where in the game's code it happened, which screen was open, the game version, the browser and system in a few words (like "Chrome 141 on macOS") and the window size. Nothing about the players, and no address or identifier, and nothing at all when "Don't count my visits" is on. A problem not seen for 180 days is deleted.

Keeping it safe. To stop people guessing save codes, we count requests from each network address for up to an hour. We keep only a scrambled version of the address, never the address itself. Sign-in cookies are stored only in scrambled form too. A player kept in a grown-up's account can only be deleted by that grown-up. Everything travels over encrypted connections, and only FancyAnt Games can reach the saved data.

Backups. Once a week we copy the saved players and grown-up accounts to private storage, so we can put things back if something goes wrong. Each copy is deleted automatically after 90 days.

Our hosting. The game, saves and backups run on Cloudflare, which handles the traffic to deliver the site, under its own privacy policy. Sign-in goes through Google, under its privacy policy. We don't use any other outside services in the game.

What we don't do

  • No advertising, and no ad or analytics trackers.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of information with anyone.
  • No chat, messages or ways for players to contact each other.
  • We don't use what's saved for anything except running the game for you.

How long we keep it

  • A saved player that nobody opens for 18 months is deleted automatically, unless it's kept in a grown-up's account.
  • A grown-up account unused for 3 years is deleted automatically.
  • Sign-ins last up to 180 days, then you sign in again.
  • An email left for a reply (Ideas and help) is deleted after 180 days; a problem report not seen for 180 days is deleted.
  • When you delete something, it's gone from our database right away, and from our weekly backups within 90 days.

Your choices

  • See what's saved: open the player with its save code; everything stored is what the game shows.
  • Delete a saved player: Grown-ups screen, "Stop saving online". Progress stays on that computer. For a player kept in your account, sign in first. Signed in, you can also delete a player everywhere (Settings, "Delete this player"): it's removed from your account and from every computer.
  • Don't count my visits: Grown-ups screen, under "Counting visits". It stops the visit counts and the automatic problem reports from that computer.
  • Delete your account: Grown-ups screen, "Delete my account". You can keep or delete your players' save codes.
  • Questions or requests (including asking us to delete something for you): email hello@fancyant.com.

Children and schools

Keypals is made for children. We collect only what the game needs, as described above, and we don't ask children for personal information. Parents can review or delete a child's saved progress at any time with the save code or from their account, or by emailing us. Schools can use Keypals without any accounts. When teachers use saving or sign-in, we use the information only to provide the game for their class, never for any other purpose.

Changes

If this notice changes, we'll update the date above. We won't start collecting new kinds of information from children without updating this notice first.
